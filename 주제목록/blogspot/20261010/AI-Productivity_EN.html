By Roche P. | October 2026 | 9 min read

Enterprise knowledge management is fractured. Teams store work in databases, documents, meeting notes, and email. When the average worker spends 30% of their day searching for information, three platforms promise different paths forward: Notion AI 3.0 (workspace agents), Mem (personal memory capture), and Glean (organization-wide search). But which one actually solves the problem for your team?

01

Notion AI 3.0: Agents Inside Your Workspace

Notion AI 3.0
Autonomous agents that create, organize, and execute work directly in Notion

Notion's September 2026 pivot transformed its writing assistant into a full agent platform. The major shift: AI no longer just writes. It creates pages, modifies databases, schedules tasks, and runs automated workflows across your entire Notion workspace.

What's New (September 2026)

The standout feature is the redesigned model picker. Notion Agents now have access to frontier models including Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, and Kimi K3, with transparent speed, intelligence, and cost indicators for each. Workspace owners control which models Agents can use, giving teams flexibility across different task complexity levels.

Custom Agents take automation further. Schedule them to run on calendar triggers, update tasks weekly, or respond to database changes in real time. AI Meeting Notes automatically transcribe meetings, summarize them, extract action items, and populate them into Notion tasks. Enterprise Search can query Notion content, the web, and connected business applications—all with cited answers.

Pricing (as of October 2026): Notion AI is included in Business plans at $20/user/month (annual commitment) or approximately $24 monthly. Custom Agents consume credits beyond standard allowances; one reported price is $10 per 1,000 credits, though this may vary by model and usage pattern.

Strengths

  • Agents can create and modify pages, databases, and tasks directly in Notion
  • Multi-model selection with transparent cost and speed metrics
  • Meeting notes transcription and task extraction built in
  • Enterprise Search queries Notion content plus connected apps
  • Highly integrated if your team already works in Notion
  • Event-based and scheduled automation for recurring workflows

Limitations

  • Best experience requires storing most work inside Notion itself
  • Requires Business or Enterprise plan; AI is not available on Free or Plus
  • Custom Agents and enterprise connectors add cost beyond base subscription
  • Organizations with fragmented knowledge outside Notion won't get full search coverage

Mem: AI-First Personal Knowledge Capture

Mem
Lightweight capture, automatic organization, and conversational retrieval for personal knowledge

Mem inverts the traditional note-taking model. Instead of forcing users to organize information into folders and notebooks, Mem captures thoughts, documents, and web clippings, then automatically organizes them using AI. Retrieval is conversational: you chat with your knowledge base rather than search for tags or file paths.

Core Model: Capture First

The design philosophy is explicitly personal and lightweight. Mem is built for individuals or small teams who prioritize capture speed over governed, company-wide structure. Voice capture, quick text entry, web clipping, and file import all flow into a single inbox. The system then uses AI to extract topics, cross-reference related notes, and build an interconnected knowledge graph.

Mem operates on an allowance model. Free tier offers limited messages and new notes; Plus ($9/month) and Pro ($29/month) tiers increase storage, message allowances, Voice Mode hours, and the number of connected integrations and custom routines. These prices are approximate as of October 2026; exact allowances should be verified against Mem's current pricing page.

Pricing (as of October 2026): Free tier (limited); Plus approximately $9/month; Pro approximately $29/month. Allowances cover messages, new notes per month, Voice Mode hours, and integrations.

Strengths

  • Minimal friction for capturing ideas—faster than traditional notes apps
  • Automatic organization by AI means less manual tagging and sorting
  • Conversational retrieval feels natural for personal knowledge search
  • Voice capture for on-the-go note-taking
  • Low-cost entry point; Pro tier at $29/month is affordable
  • Purpose-built for personal knowledge, not enterprise governance

Limitations

  • Not designed for large-scale, company-wide knowledge management
  • Limited enterprise connectors compared to Glean
  • Allowance-based model can feel restrictive if you capture heavily
  • No public benchmarks on retrieval accuracy or context quality
  • Smaller ecosystem; fewer third-party integrations than Notion
  • Best suited for individual use, not collaborative team workflows

Glean: Enterprise-Wide Search and Knowledge Assistant

Glean
Permissions-aware search and AI assistant for organization-wide knowledge retrieval

Glean operates in a different category entirely. It's not a workspace or a personal notes app—it's a knowledge and search platform purpose-built for large enterprises. Glean connects to your entire organization's data: Slack, email, Google Workspace, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Jira, GitHub, and dozens of business applications. A single AI assistant searches across all of them with permission-aware answers.

Enterprise-First Design

The core problem Glean solves is organizational fragmentation. When knowledge is scattered across dozens of tools, employees spend hours searching for answers. Glean indexes everything, learns organizational structure and access policies, and delivers answers that respect who should see what. If you don't have access to a document, Glean won't show it to you—even if it contains the answer.

Enterprise connectors, governance frameworks, deployment options (cloud or on-premise), and dedicated support are built in, not added later. Glean requires a contract; there is no self-serve pricing.

Pricing (as of October 2026): No published list price. Public purchasing data suggests median annual contracts near $98,000–$99,000, with typical per-user estimates around $25–$40/month depending on seat count and deployment complexity. Glean is a contract negotiation, not a subscription.

Strengths

  • Searches across your entire organization's knowledge base in one place
  • Permissions-aware results prevent unauthorized data access
  • Broad enterprise connectors (Slack, email, Salesforce, Jira, GitHub, etc.)
  • Governance, compliance, and audit capabilities built in
  • Deployment flexibility (cloud or on-premise)
  • Dedicated enterprise support and implementation team
  • Purpose-built for large organizations with complex knowledge infrastructure

Limitations

  • Enterprise sales process and contract negotiation required
  • Significantly higher cost than Notion or Mem ($98k+ annually)
  • Overkill for small teams or startups
  • Longer implementation time compared to self-serve tools
  • Vendor lock-in common with enterprise contracts
  • No public benchmarks comparing its search accuracy to competitors

Benchmark Reality Check

As of October 2026, there is no published, controlled benchmark that tests all three tools on the same dataset using identical metrics. Product claims about retrieval accuracy, answer hallucination rates, or permission compliance are not independently validated through apples-to-apples testing.

The available evidence comes from:

  • + Official product documentation: Notion's feature announcements are detailed and verifiable; same for Glean's integration lists.
  • + Independent pricing and positioning reviews: Useful for comparing cost and intended use case, but not for performance metrics.
  • + Enterprise purchasing data: Useful for understanding Glean's typical contract size, but not for answer quality or speed.

If accuracy, speed, or retrieval quality matter to your decision, test all three on a representative sample of your own questions and measure precision, recall, citation accuracy, and latency before buying.

Final Verdict: Which Tool for Which Scenario

  • Choose Notion AI 3.0 if: Your team already works in Notion and you want AI agents that can create pages, organize databases, run scheduled workflows, and transcribe meetings. Best for small to mid-size teams (under 200 people) committed to Notion as their operating system.
  • Choose Mem if: You're an individual or very small team that prioritizes rapid capture and conversational retrieval over governance. You don't need enterprise features; you need a fast, AI-organized personal brain.
  • Choose Glean if: Your organization has 500+ employees, knowledge scattered across 10+ business systems, and you need permission-aware search with enterprise deployment support. You have a budget for enterprise software and a 6-12 week implementation timeline.

Bottom Line

Notion AI 3.0 is the strongest workspace-integrated option. Its September 2026 agent update and multi-model picker give it real automation muscle, but it only shines if your team is already in Notion.

Mem is the fastest personal knowledge tool. Capture is effortless, organization is automatic, and pricing is affordable. It's not for teams; it's for people who want to stop thinking about file structure.

Glean is the most powerful enterprise search platform. If your organization needs organization-wide knowledge retrieval with permissions and governance, Glean is the category leader. The cost and complexity are justified only at scale.

The question isn't "which is best"—it's "which problem am I solving?" Answer that first, and the choice becomes obvious.
