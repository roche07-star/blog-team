Email drowns most professionals in a sea of noise. With the average knowledge worker receiving 126+ emails per day, the question isn't whether you need help managing your inbox—it's which AI tool actually saves you time. Let's break down what actually works.

01

Superhuman Mail: Speed-Obsessed Email Workflows

S

Superhuman Mail

Email client built for keyboard power users and AI-assisted workflows

Superhuman positions itself as the speed champion. In September 2026, the platform rolled out a comprehensive redesign with new iOS/Android apps and desktop themes, including performance improvements that the company claims deliver 2x faster AI writing and voice features. This is a vendor claim rather than an independent benchmark, but the emphasis on velocity reflects Superhuman's core philosophy: faster email means more time saved.

The platform's strength lies in email-first automation. Features include auto-labeling, auto-archiving, and AI-drafted replies that pull context from your inbox, calendar, web browsing, and even Slack. The new "Ask AI" sidebar on desktop extends this beyond email—it's become a task-oriented assistant that understands your workflow. Superhuman has also launched into adjacent products: Superhuman Docs (with AI drafting, tables, custom views, and database support) and Superhuman Go (a mobile-first workspace experience), expanding from email into broader productivity.

The integration with custom agents is significant. Superhuman now supports workflow automation that feels more autonomous than traditional email templates. If you live in your inbox and want aggressive AI assistance across the entire email lifecycle—composing, routing, and responding—this is the native choice.

Best For

Email power users who embrace keyboard shortcuts, fast email volume, and want AI assistance baked into every email action. Ideal if you're already a Superhuman user and want to expand into docs and automation.

02

Grammarly (Now Superhuman): Writing Assistance Within the Suite

G

Grammarly

Writing quality and tone assistant, now integrated into Superhuman platform

Grammarly's major September 2026 announcement: the company rebranded as Superhuman, consolidating under one platform. This is a strategic shift. Grammarly—once a standalone grammar and writing tool—is now the writing-focused module within the broader Superhuman suite, alongside mail, docs, and agents.

What does this mean for users? Grammarly's core strength remains unchanged: writing quality, editing suggestions, and tone detection. The platform catches grammar, punctuation, and clarity issues in real time, and allows users to adjust tone (formal, conversational, confident, etc.) for different email contexts. In a professional email setting, this is valuable. A draft that sounds aggressive can be softened before hitting send.

However, from the available September 2026 sources, Grammarly is no longer a standalone email product. It's a writing assistant that works within Superhuman's ecosystem. If you're looking for a dedicated email management tool, Grammarly is not it. But if you need writing assistance across email, docs, and other content creation as part of a larger productivity suite, it's the writing layer of the Superhuman platform.

The company also acquired GPTZero in 2026, suggesting an expanded focus on AI detection and authenticity in writing, though full details on integration are still emerging.

Pricing (as of September 2026)

Specific pricing details are not provided in the latest public sources. Grammarly pricing historically varies by plan (Free, Premium, Business), but confirmation of September 2026 rates requires direct verification.

Best For

Teams focused on writing quality and tone consistency across all written communication. Ideal if you're adopting Superhuman as a full suite and want integrated writing assistance rather than a separate tool.

03

Google Gemini: General-Purpose AI Across Your Workspace

G

Google Gemini

General-purpose AI assistant integrated across Google products

Note: Official September 2026 pricing and feature announcements for Gemini are not included in the provided sources. The following assessment is based on Gemini's general positioning as of mid-2026.

Google Gemini takes a different approach than Superhuman's email-first model. Instead of building a specialized email product, Google embeds Gemini—its large language model—across Gmail, Google Docs, Google Calendar, and Google Workspace. This is a integrated ecosystem play, not a standalone email client.

Within Gmail specifically, Gemini can help draft emails and suggest responses, but the integration is less aggressive than Superhuman's. You're not getting a redesigned interface optimized for keyboard shortcuts or high-volume email handling. Instead, you get AI assistance layered on top of Gmail's familiar interface. For users already deep in Google's ecosystem (Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Meet), Gemini adds value across all surfaces. For email-obsessed power users, it may feel less specialized.

The advantage: cost and ecosystem integration. If you're already paying for Google Workspace, Gemini access may be included or a modest add-on. There's no need to switch email clients or adopt an entirely new productivity platform. The disadvantage: Gemini is designed to be general-purpose, not obsessively optimized for email like Superhuman.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose Superhuman Mail if...

You receive 50+ emails daily and live in your inbox. You want AI that drafts replies, auto-routes messages, and integrates with your calendar and web browsing. You're willing to migrate email clients to gain speed and automation. You're open to expanding into their docs and agent ecosystem. Best for: Investment managers, sales teams, journalists, customer support specialists.

Choose Grammarly if...

Your primary pain point is writing quality, not email volume. You want to improve tone and clarity across all written communication (email, Slack, docs, posts). You prefer a lightweight tool that doesn't require platform migration. You're comfortable with it as one layer of a broader productivity workflow. Best for: Marketing teams, content creators, remote-first companies, anyone who writes frequently.

Choose Google Gemini if...

You're already deeply embedded in Google Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Calendar, Meet). You want AI assistance without switching email clients or adopting new tools. You prioritize integration and minimal setup over specialized email features. You value cost-effectiveness and ecosystem consistency. Best for: Organizations using Google Workspace, freelancers on Google, startups avoiding tool sprawl.

The Verdict

Superhuman vs. Grammarly vs. Gemini: Final Thoughts

There's no single winner in September 2026. Each tool addresses email productivity differently:

  • Superhuman Mail is the specialist—built from the ground up for email velocity and AI-assisted workflows. If inbox management is your bottleneck, it delivers.
  • Grammarly is the writing layer. Its integration into Superhuman's broader suite doesn't make it an email tool, but it strengthens writing quality across all platforms.
  • Google Gemini is the integrator. For organizations already on Google Workspace, it's the path of least resistance and delivers solid AI assistance without platform friction.

The best choice depends on your workflow, not the tools themselves. High-volume email? Superhuman. Writing-first workflow? Grammarly. Google ecosystem locked-in? Gemini. The trend in September 2026 is clear: AI email assistance is table stakes. The differentiation is speed, specialization, and integration depth.
